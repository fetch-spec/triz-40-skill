# The 40 Inventive Principles as Questions

Ask every one, in order. For each: give a concrete suggestion, or write "nothing useful here".

| # | Principle | Question | Plain explanation |
|---|-----------|----------|-------------------|
| 1 | Segmentation | Can we divide it into independent, modular, or sectional parts? | Break one big thing into smaller pieces that can change on their own. |
| 2 | Taking out (extraction) | Can we remove the troublesome part or property, or keep only the useful one? | Pull out what causes the trouble, or isolate just what you need. |
| 3 | Local quality | Can different zones or parts follow different rules suited to their job? | Not everything needs the same treatment; tune each area to its role. |
| 4 | Asymmetry | Would an asymmetric or one-way shape, layout, or flow work better? | Break symmetry when the two sides have different needs. |
| 5 | Merging (consolidation) | Can we combine identical or adjacent parts or operations? | Do similar things together, with one mechanism. |
| 6 | Universality | Can one part do several jobs, so others can be removed? | Make one element multi-purpose. |
| 7 | Nested doll | Can one object or idea sit inside another? | Contain things within things to save space or keep context. |
| 8 | Anti-weight (counterweight) | Can another force or resource offset the load? | Let something else carry part of the burden. |
| 9 | Preliminary anti-action | Can we apply the opposite effect in advance to cancel a harm later? | Head off a known problem before it starts. |
| 10 | Preliminary action | Can we do the needed change, fully or partly, in advance? | Prepare ahead so the real step is quick. |
| 11 | Beforehand cushioning | Can we prepare a backup or safe landing for a likely failure? | Plan for failure so it does little damage. |
| 12 | Equipotentiality | Can we avoid repeated lifting, lowering, or converting across the process? | Keep things at one level so you don't keep re-working them. |
| 13 | The other way round (inversion) | Can we invert the action, fix what moves, or move what's fixed? | Flip the problem: pull instead of push, start from the other end. |
| 14 | Spheroidality / curvature | Can straight or flat become curved, rotary, or cyclic? | Swap linear for round or looping forms. |
| 15 | Dynamics | Can parts or parameters adapt as conditions change? | Make it adjustable instead of fixed. |
| 16 | Partial or excessive action | If 100% is hard, would slightly less or more be much easier? | Aim a bit under or over the target, then correct. |
| 17 | Another dimension | Can we add a layer, plane, or level, or use the other side? | Move into a new dimension to escape crowding. |
| 18 | Mechanical vibration | Can oscillation, frequency, or resonance help? | Use vibration or repetition as a tool. |
| 19 | Periodic action | Can continuous action become pulsed, or use the pauses? | Do it in bursts or on a schedule. |
| 20 | Continuity of useful action | Can everything work usefully all the time, with no idle runs? | Remove waste and dead time. |
| 21 | Skipping (rushing through) | Can a harmful stage be passed so fast the harm can't develop? | Get through the bad part quickly. |
| 22 | Blessing in disguise | Can a harmful factor be turned into a benefit? | Use the problem itself as a resource. |
| 23 | Feedback | Can we add or tune feedback? | Measure results and feed them back to steer. |
| 24 | Intermediary | Can an intermediate object or carrier do the job? | Put a go-between in the middle. |
| 25 | Self-service | Can it serve, check, repair, or regulate itself? | Let the system look after itself. |
| 26 | Copying | Can a cheap copy, model, or sample replace the costly original? | Test or work on a stand-in. |
| 27 | Cheap short-living objects | Can cheap, disposable things replace an expensive durable one? | Use throwaway versions to save cost or risk. |
| 28 | Mechanics substitution | Can we replace a manual or mechanical method with another field (sensing, automation, software)? | Swap the mechanism for a smarter one. |
| 29 | Pneumatics and hydraulics | Can a fluid, flexible, or flowing element replace a rigid one? | Replace solid with something that flows or cushions. |
| 30 | Flexible shells and thin films | Can a thin flexible layer replace a rigid structure or isolate it? | Wrap or separate with a thin layer. |
| 31 | Porous materials | Can we use holes, gaps, or empty space? | Make the gaps useful. |
| 32 | Color changes | Can a change of color, transparency, or visibility help? | Make status easy to see. |
| 33 | Homogeneity | Should interacting things be made of the same (or compatible) stuff? | Match the parts that touch each other. |
| 34 | Discarding and recovering | Can a part disappear after use, or be restored during operation? | Throw away or rebuild instead of preserving forever. |
| 35 | Parameter changes | Can we change state, concentration, flexibility, or another property? | Change the properties of the thing itself. |
| 36 | Phase transitions | Can a change of phase or state do useful work? | Use transitions between states. |
| 37 | Thermal expansion | Can expansion, growth, or differing rates be used? | Use how things grow or shrink with conditions. |
| 38 | Strong oxidants (enriched atmosphere) | Can an enriched or more active environment help? | Boost the surroundings to speed things up. |
| 39 | Inert atmosphere | Can an inert, isolated, or protected environment remove the harm? | Shield the work from what damages it. |
| 40 | Composite materials | Can a combination replace a uniform material or single method? | Combine several sources or methods for strength. |
